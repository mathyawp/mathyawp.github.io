Why it breaks

Heights fall, slopes climb

Per-term amplitude vs. steepest slope, for the current a and b.
aⁿ → 0  (sum converges ⇒ continuous)
(ab)ⁿ → ∞  (slopes diverge ⇒ not differentiable)
How to use this
The idea. Each cosine term is perfectly smooth. Summing more of them keeps the curve bounded — heights decay like aⁿ — yet makes it ever more jagged, because the steepest slope of term n grows like (ab)ⁿ.

Try this. Drag Terms up slowly and watch detail appear at finer and finer scales. Then zoom in (scroll) anywhere — the roughness never resolves into a smooth arc.

Self-similarity. Replacing x by x/b and scaling height by a returns a similar picture. To see it, zoom width by about b and height by about 1/a (use Shift+scroll and Alt+scroll).

Slope probe. Turn it on and click the curve. You get the true derivative of the partial sum W′N there — and the inset shows |W′j| exploding as j grows, which is exactly why no finite limiting slope exists.

At extreme settings the structure becomes finer than a pixel (and finer than floating-point can hold). That limit is the point, not a bug.
