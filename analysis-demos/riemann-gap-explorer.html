Why refinement works, and where it stops working

Two ways to make a term small. Shrink the oscillation — a continuous f on a short interval — or shrink the weight, where α barely moves. Try Only the middle and watch the outer intervals stop contributing entirely.

Refinement never hurts. Adding a point can only lower U and raise L, so the gap never grows. Split largest term cuts the worst interval in half; the common refinement P ∪ Q is at least as good as either partition on its own.

Where it fails. Choose Dirichlet f. Every nondegenerate interval contains both rationals and irrationals, so Mi − mi = 1 however fine P becomes. The shaded area fills the whole box: the gap is stuck at 1, and f is not integrable.
